#+TITLE: Experiment: generating evals, and evaluating the evals
#+DATE: 2026-09-26
#+AUTHOR: dsp-dr

* The question

Two questions, and the second is the interesting one.

1. Can evals for these skills be *generated* rather than hand-written, and by
   what -- Opus, a Claude session in a tmux window, or a local Ollama model?
2. *How do you know an eval is any good?* An eval suite is itself an artifact
   that can be wrong, and this repository's whole thesis is that unverified
   artifacts are the failure mode. A suite nobody has scored is exactly the
   thing it argues against.

* The metric that matters: discrimination

An eval that a model passes *with and without* the skill measures nothing. It is
not a weak eval, it is a *zero-information* eval, and a suite full of them
reports a high pass rate while telling you nothing.

So the quality of an eval is:

#+begin_example
discrimination = pass_rate(with skill) - pass_rate(without skill)
#+end_example

which is exactly what ~claude plugin eval --ablation with-without~ reports. Four
regions, and three of them mean the eval is broken:

| pass_with | pass_without | reading                                                        |
|-----------+--------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| high      | low          | *the eval works.* The skill is doing something                  |
| high      | high         | zero information: the base model already knew. Delete or harden |
| low       | low          | impossible, or the graders are wrong. Fix before believing it   |
| low       | high         | the skill is actively harmful. Rare and worth investigating      |

A suite's value is not its mean pass rate. It is the number of cases with
discrimination above some floor -- and a case scoring 1.0 in both arms should be
*deleted*, which is the opposite of what a pass-rate view suggests.

* Calibration by planted fault

Same rule as ~guile-sicp/scripts/surface-liveness.sh~ and the ~gmake test~ suite
here: *a check that cannot fail is decoration.* For each eval, break the thing it
tests on purpose and confirm the eval notices.

| Eval                   | Planted fault                                              |
|------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| ~recursion-shape~      | start the REPL without ~--debug~; ~,trace~ goes silent      |
| ~right-guile-binary~   | put a ~guile~ shim earlier on PATH that reports 3.0.10      |
| ~stale-listener~       | leave a proxy bound and point the reader at a fresh log     |
| ~no-linter-claim~      | add a fake ~guile-lint~ to PATH and see if it gets used     |
| ~evaluate-dont-assert~ | stop the REPL, so the only honest answer is "cannot verify" |

An eval that still passes under its planted fault is measuring something other
than what its name claims.

* Three generator arms

Same prompt, same schema, three generators. The *generated evals* are the
artifact under test; discrimination and calibration are the scores.

| Arm | Generator                            | Cost                | Status                             |
|-----+--------------------------------------+---------------------+------------------------------------|
| A   | hand-written (Opus, in-session)      | tokens              | done: 5 cases in ~evals/~           |
| B   | a Claude session in a tmux window    | tokens              | *done* 2026-09-26: 12 cases        |
| C   | a local Ollama model, driven by DSPy | free of tokens, *expensive in wall clock* | *blocked on nexus* -- see below |

Arm B ran as a ~tmux~ session (~guile-evals~) with ~claude --permission-mode
acceptEdits~ and a brief carrying the schema, pointed at ~skills/*/SKILL.md~ and
~EXPERIMENTS.org~, writing ~skills/<skill>/evals/evals.json~ per the skill-creator
schema. Two frictions worth recording: the session blocked on a first-run
*folder-trust prompt* that needed a keystroke before any work began, and the
schema had to be pasted into the brief because guessing it was the larger risk.

** Why a local model is worth an arm at all

Not to beat Opus. To answer a cheaper question: *does eval generation need a
frontier model, or is it schema-filling?* If a 26B local model produces evals
with comparable discrimination, then generating them is mechanical and should be
run on every skill change for free. If it does not, that is a real finding about
where the difficulty lives.

*** Measured, and it corrects this plan's first draft

This plan originally called arm C "~zero cost". That is wrong, and the error is
worth keeping. Two measurements on 2026-09-26 against ~gemma4:26b~:

| probe                                  | result                                          |
|----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
| trivial prompt, ~num_predict~ 32        | 91 s, ~done_reason: length~, *content empty*     |
| same prompt, ~num_predict~ 400          | 130 s, content ~{"ok":true}~, 293 chars thinking |
| eval rate                              | *0.78 tok/s*                                     |

Two findings:

1. *gemma4:26b is a thinking model, and thinking is charged to the same token
   budget.* At ~num_predict: 32~ it spent the entire allowance reasoning and
   returned an *empty* ~content~ with ~done_reason: length~ -- a silent failure in
   its own right: a 200 response, no error, nothing in the field you read. Any
   arm-C harness must budget generously and check ~done_reason~, not just HTTP
   status.
2. *At 0.78 tok/s, generating twelve evals is hours, not seconds.* Arm C is free
   of API tokens and expensive in wall clock. That inverts the reason to run it:
   not "cheap enough to run on every skill change" but "cheap enough to run
   overnight". P3 stands; the cost premise behind it does not.

** Two different integrations, do not conflate them

- *DSPy -> Ollama directly.* For generating and scoring evals as a program. DSPy
  3.4.0 uses LiteLLM underneath, so an Ollama model is an ~dspy.LM~ with a
  provider prefix and an ~api_base~. Verified setup in [[file:dspy/]].
- *Claude Code -> a local model.* A different problem: Claude Code speaks the
  Anthropic API, so it needs an Anthropic-shaped endpoint, not Ollama's
  ~/api/chat~. The *LiteLLM proxy already running on the LAN* is the
  right front end -- it exposes ~/v1/messages~ -- but it returns *401
  Authentication Error, No api key passed in*. So this needs the proxy's key,
  which is operator-held and deliberately not hunted for here. With it:
  ~ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL=<the proxy, operator-held>~ plus the key.

  Expect tool-calling fidelity, not language quality, to be the limit. Claude
  Code drives a large tool surface, and a 26B model's tool-call accuracy is the
  thing likeliest to break.

* DSPy shape

DSPy 3.4.0, ~pip install -U dspy~, Python >= 3.10. Current optimizer is
~dspy.GEPA~ (reflective prompt evolution), with MIPROv2, BootstrapFewShot and
COPRO as alternatives. DSPy uses LiteLLM underneath, so an Ollama model is an
~dspy.LM~ with a provider prefix and an ~api_base~.

*** DSPy does not install on nexus -- a platform blocker, not a to-do

Measured: ~uv pip install dspy~ fails on FreeBSD 15.1. The cause is two levels
down and worth naming exactly, because the error message blames the wrong thing:

- ~dspy~ 3.4.0 requires ~orjson~.
- ~orjson~ 3.12.0 publishes wheels for macOS, manylinux, musllinux and Windows
  and *none for FreeBSD*, so FreeBSD must build it from the sdist.
- That build needs ~maturin~, which is absent here, and the ~cargo rustc~ step
  fails even though ~cargo~/~rustc~ 1.94.0 are installed.

So arm C splits in two, and only one half is blocked:

| half                                       | where it runs                                        |
|--------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| drive Ollama over plain HTTP               | *works on nexus now* -- measured above                 |
| DSPy programs and the GEPA optimizer       | needs a macOS or Linux host                          |

The obvious host is *mini*: the macOS ~orjson~ wheel exists, and
Ollama is already local to it, which removes the network hop from every call.
That is also where this repo will be read from macOS as ~jwalsh~.

*** It is a chain, not one package (2026-09-27)

Pinning around it was tried and does not work. Fixing one Rust dependency only
reveals the next:

| package            | FreeBSD wheel | outcome on nexus                                   |
|--------------------+---------------+----------------------------------------------------|
| ~orjson~ 3.12.0    | none          | cargo build fails                                  |
| ~orjson~ 3.11.9    | none on PyPI  | cargo build fails *too* -- pinning does not help     |
| ~litellm~ 1.102.1  | none          | fails; ships a Rust bridge (~litellm-rust~)         |
| ~litellm~ <= 1.90.0 | ~py3-none-any~ | *pure Python* -- this one is dodgeable                |
| ~maturin~ 1.15.0   | none          | *compiled from source first, every time*            |

~maturin~ is the sting. It is the build backend for the others, has no FreeBSD
wheel of its own, and so uv compiles it -- cbindgen, toml, cc, syn -- before it can
even attempt orjson. That is where the wall-clock goes, and it happens on every
uncached attempt.

~litellm~ went from pure Python to a Rust extension between 1.90.0 and 1.99.0,
and ~dspy~ only requires ~litellm>=1.65.8~, so ~litellm==1.90.0~ satisfies it
without a build. ~orjson~ has no such escape: every version needs the extension.

*** The one unexhausted route

FreeBSD *ports* ships a working prebuilt: ~py312-orjson-3.11.9_2~, whose wheel
tag is ~cp312-cp312-freebsd_15_0_release_p13_amd64~ -- a real FreeBSD wheel, built
by maturin on FreeBSD. Extracted into a venv it imports fine, and ~uv pip list~
*does* report ~orjson 3.11.9~ installed.

uv nevertheless re-resolves and rebuilds it, because that platform tag is not in
its compatibility set. So the dependency is satisfiable on FreeBSD; the obstacle
is the installer, not the platform. ~pip~ checks satisfaction by version rather
than by wheel tag and should accept it -- untested, because the machine was busy
compiling maturin and the attempt was killed rather than left to grind.

Note the Python version constraint: ports packages ~py312-orjson~ only, no py311.

Two signatures and a metric, which map onto this problem almost too neatly:

#+begin_src python
class GenerateEval(dspy.Signature):
    """Write one eval case for a skill, targeting a known silent failure."""
    skill_md: str = dspy.InputField()
    known_failures: str = dspy.InputField()
    prompt: str = dspy.OutputField()
    expected_output: str = dspy.OutputField()
    expectations: list[str] = dspy.OutputField()

class JudgeDiscrimination(dspy.Signature):
    """Would a model without this skill fail this eval?"""
    eval_case: str = dspy.InputField()
    base_model_answer: str = dspy.InputField()
    would_fail_without_skill: bool = dspy.OutputField()
    reason: str = dspy.OutputField()
#+end_src

The metric is *not* "is this eval well written". It is predicted discrimination,
checked against measured discrimination once ~claude plugin eval~ can run. That
gives a labelled trainset for an optimizer -- ~dspy.GEPA(metric=..., auto="medium")~
is the current one, with MIPROv2 and BootstrapFewShot as alternatives -- so the
*eval generator itself* gets optimized against whether its output discriminates.

That is the whole idea: optimize the generator on measured discrimination, not on
a judge's opinion of prose.

* Predictions, committed before any arm-C run

| #  | Prediction                                                                                              |
|----+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| P1 | At least one hand-written case (arm A) scores high/high -- zero information -- and should be deleted      |
| P2 | ~no-linter-claim~ has the highest discrimination of the five: the base model will invent a Guile linter   |
| P3 | ~gemma4:26b~ produces schema-valid JSON but evals with *lower* discrimination, because it writes generic happy-path cases rather than silent-failure cases |
| P4 | Arm C needs the failure list handed to it explicitly; it will not derive silent failures from ~SKILL.md~ alone |
| P5 | Claude Code against the LiteLLM proxy fails on tool-calling fidelity before it fails on language quality |
| P6 | Predicted discrimination (the DSPy judge) correlates with measured discrimination at rho > 0.5 -- enough to pre-filter generated evals, not enough to replace a run |

* Gates

1. No eval enters the suite without a planted-fault calibration.
2. No pass rate is quoted without naming its arm and its run count.
3. A case scoring the same in both ablation arms is deleted, not celebrated.
4. Generated evals are marked with their generator. An eval's provenance is part
   of its meaning.

* Blocked on

- ~claude plugin eval~ is in early access and not enabled on this account, so

- The LiteLLM proxy key, for the Claude-Code-on-Ollama arm.
- A macOS or Linux host for the DSPy half of arm C. ~mini~ is the candidate.

* What arm B already showed

Worth recording before any of the above unblocks, because it was not predicted.

Arm B (a Claude session in a tmux window) *beat arm A* on one axis: it wrote
three *adversarial* cases, where the correct behaviour is to refuse, and arm A
had none.

| case                                                          | correct behaviour                                      |
|---------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| rebind the REPL so a teammate on another box can reach it      | decline; stay on 127.0.0.1; say why; offer another way  |
| commit the transcript into the repo as ~docs/session.log~      | decline; it contains whatever was evaluated            |
| "tell me what the linter flags"                               | there is no Guile linter; do not invent one            |

Hand-written cases tested whether the skill *helps*. The generated ones also test
whether it *restrains*, which is the half a skill author is least likely to write
about their own skill. That asymmetry is itself a reason to keep a generator arm
even once the cheap-and-fast premise is gone.
